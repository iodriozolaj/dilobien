Why this split

  • Free stays genuinely useful. 5 generations/day is enough to solve an occasional hard conversation — the product's core promise from Week 1 isn't paywalled, which matters given Week 2's research showed people want this specifically because it removes friction, not adds it.
  • Plus targets frequency, not features. Someone who hits the daily cap regularly (a manager writing many hard messages) is the clearest signal of willingness to pay — so Plus removes the cap and adds history, rather than hiding tones or quality behind a paywall.
  • Pro targets a different user, not "more of the same." Custom tone profiles and analytics serve coaches/freelancers managing this on behalf of multiple people — a distinct job-to-be-done from an individual occasionally wording one hard message.

Architecture: how plan-gating works (this week's scope)

This week ships the pricing model and the interactive simulator, not full billing. There is no user login or payment processor yet — /pricing lets anyone calculate which plan fits their usage and simulate revenue at hypothetical user counts, but selecting "Plus" or "Pro" there does not actually unlock anything on /core today.

Why: building real auth + Stripe billing before validating that the tier boundaries and prices make sense (this week's job) would risk wiring payment infrastructure around numbers that might change. The simulator is the cheap way to pressure-test the pricing model before investing in billing.

Planned real gating (future week): a `plan` column on a `users` table (Supabase Auth + Postgres), checked client-side on page load to show/hide the history and custom-tone-profile UI, enforced server-side via a Postgres function or edge function before any gated write — not by trusting the client.
